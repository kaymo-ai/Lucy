## **Infrastructure and Engineering: Shelter and Anchoring**

### **Anchoring to the Playa**

The playa surface is hard-packed silt that can behave like concrete. Traditional tent stakes often fail in high winds (60+ mph gusts).

* **Lag Bolts (The Gold Standard):**  
  * **Spec:** 3/8" diameter x 14" (or 18") length lag screws.  
  * **Installation:** Requires an impact driver. Do not attempt to hammer them in.  
  * **Chain Link:** Use two links of heavy chain on the bolt head to attach guy lines.  
  * **Removal:** Simply reverse the impact driver. Leaves minimal MOOP/damage compared to rebar.  
* **Rebar (The Old Way):**  
  * **Risk:** "Rebar Foot" (tripping on exposed rebar) is a common injury. Must be capped with tennis balls or mushrooms.  
  * **Removal:** extremely difficult; often requires vice grips and spinning to break the suction.  
* **Candy Canes:** Rebar bent into a U-shape to prevent impalement.

### **Shade Structures**

You generally cannot sleep past 8:00 AM in a tent without shade due to the greenhouse effect.

* **Aluminet:** A knitted aluminum fabric that reflects heat while allowing airflow (unlike tarps). 70% shade density is standard.  
* **Monkey Hut:** A DIY shade structure made from PVC pipes bent into a quonset hut shape, covered with a tarp. Cheap, effective, and wind-resistant if anchored properly.  
* **Conduit Flat Top:** A boxy structure built from EMT conduit and connector fittings. Stronger than PVC.

### **Housing Types**

* **Hexayurt:** A rigid shelter made from insulation boards (usually R-Max Thermasheath) taped together.  
  * **Pros:** Keeps dust out, stays cool, dark.  
  * **Cons:** Bulky to transport; produces immense MOOP if the tape shreds.  
* **Shiftpod:** A commercial reflective insulated tent designed specifically for Burning Man. Expensive but sets up in minutes.

## **The 10 Principles and Cultural Norms**

### **The 10 Principles (Summary for Vector Search)**

1. **Radical Inclusion:** Anyone may be a part of Burning Man. No prerequisites.  
2. **Gifting:** The value of a gift is unconditional. Gifting does not contemplate a return or an exchange for something of equal value. **It is NOT bartering.**  
3. **Decommodification:** No commercial sponsorships, transactions, or advertising. You cannot buy or sell anything in BRC (except ice and coffee at Center Camp).  
4. **Radical Self-Reliance:** You are responsible for your own subsistence.  
5. **Radical Self-Expression:** Arises from the unique gifts of the individual.  
6. **Communal Effort:** Creative cooperation and collaboration.  
7. **Civic Responsibility:** Valuing civil society and obeying local/federal laws.  
8. **Leaving No Trace (LNT):** Clean up after yourself. Leave the place better than you found it.  
9. **Participation:** Transformative change happens through doing. No spectators.  
10. **Immediacy:** Overcoming barriers that stand between us and a recognition of our inner selves and the reality of those around us.

### **Cultural Concepts**

* **Consent:** Essential in all interactions. "Ask first" applies to hugging, photography, and gifting food/drink.  
* **Default World:** The world outside of Burning Man.  
* **Decompression:** The period of psychological adjustment after returning to the Default World (often characterized by sadness or life re-evaluation).  
* **MOOP Map:** A map released after the event by the Playa Restoration Team showing which camps left trash behind. "Red" on the map is a badge of shame and can jeopardize future placement.

## **Logistics: Ticketing and Entry**

### **Ticketing Phases**

* **FOMO Sale:** High-priced pre-sale tickets (usually \~$1500–$2500).  
* **Stewards Sale (DGS):** Directed Group Sale. Allocated to established Theme Camps and Art Projects. Not open to the general public.  
* **Main Sale:** The general lottery. Extremely low odds of success.  
* **STEP (Secure Ticket Exchange Program):** The official platform to buy/sell face-value tickets safely.  
* **OMG Sale:** A final, small batch of tickets released just before the event.

### **Passes and Permits**

* **Vehicle Pass (VP):** Every vehicle entering BRC requires a pass (sticker) on the windshield. No pass \= no entry.  
* **WAP (Work Access Pass):** Allows early entry (Build Week) for artists and camp builders.  
* **Mutant Vehicle License:** Required to drive an art car. Issued by the DMV (Department of Mutant Vehicles).

### **Transportation**

* **Burner Express Bus (BXB):** A shuttle bus from SF/Reno.  
  * **Advantage:** Bypasses the main Gate traffic (shorter wait). Includes water allocation.  
* **Gate Road:** The multi-lane dirt road from the paved highway (Rt 34\) to the Greeters station. Speed limit is 10 MPH. Traffic ("Pulse") can last 6–12 hours.

## **Health, Safety, and Law Enforcement**

### **Law Enforcement (LEO)**

Burning Man takes place on **Federal Land**. State and Federal laws apply.

* **Agencies:** BLM Rangers (Federal), Pershing County Sheriffs (Local), undercover officers.  
* **Common Citations:** Drug possession, public urination, serving alcohol to minors, driving under the influence (even on a bike).  
* **Cannabis:** While legal in Nevada state, it is **illegal** on federal land (BLM). Possession can lead to a federal ticket.

### **Medical Emergencies**

* **ESD (Emergency Services Department):** Professional medical staff.  
* **Rampart:** The main field hospital/ICU on playa.  
* **Sanctuary:** A space managed by Zendo Project (or Green Dot Rangers) for people undergoing difficult psychedelic experiences. They provide "sitters," not medical intervention.

### **Environmental Hazards**

* **Whiteout:** A dust storm with near-zero visibility.  
  * **Action:** Stop immediately. Do not drive or bike. If walking, sit down. Orient yourself using the sound of the nearest sound camp if visual landmarks are gone.  
* **Playa Lung:** Respiratory irritation from dust.  
  * **Remedy:** N95 mask, saline nasal spray, Neti pot.

## **Technical Systems: Power and Communications**

### **Power Grids**

There is no city power. All electricity is generated locally.

* **Generators:** Honda EU series (2200i) are the standard for small camps due to quiet operation (50dB).  
  * **Baffle Box:** A DIY box lined with sound foam to reduce generator noise further.  
* **Solar:** Increasing popularity. Requires MPPT controllers and LiFePO4 batteries. Dust accumulation on panels significantly reduces efficiency (must be wiped daily).  
* **Grounding:** Generators must be grounded if part of a larger distribution grid.

### **Communications**

Cell service is spotty to non-existent.

* **WiFi:** Some camps provide public WiFi, but it is unreliable.  
* **Mesh Networks:** GoTenna and similar LoRa-based mesh devices are popular for intra-camp communication.  
* **GMRS/FRS Radios:** Walkie-talkies are common.  
  * **Channel 5:** Often monitored by emergency services (unofficially).  
  * **Channel 7:** Often used by Gate/Perimeter (listen only).

### **Grey Water Evaporation Systems**

* **Evapotron:** A device that uses a pump to spray grey water onto a spinning mesh or vertical surface to speed up evaporation.  
* **Pond:** A framed sheet of black plastic. The black color absorbs heat to aid evaporation. Needs to be shallow (1 inch depth is ideal).
