## **Burning Man Glossary: Acronyms and Entities**

This section defines common terms, acronyms, and organizational entities found within Black Rock City (BRC).

### **Acronyms**

* **BRC (Black Rock City):** The temporary city built by participants; the official name of the event location.  
* **BLM (Bureau of Land Management):** The US federal agency that manages the Black Rock Desert. They enforce federal laws and issue the event permit.  
* **DPW (Department of Public Works):** The workforce responsible for building the physical infrastructure of the city (roads, fence, power grid) before the event and restoring the desert afterwards. Known for a gruff, hard-working culture.  
* **MOOP (Matter Out of Place):** Any item, trash, or debris that does not belong in the Black Rock Desert. "Leave No Trace" requires removing all MOOP.  
* **ESD (Emergency Services Department):** Provides fire protection, medical services, and crisis intervention.  
* **DMV (Department of Mutant Vehicles):** The organization that licenses Art Cars (Mutant Vehicles) to drive on the open playa.  
* **LNT (Leave No Trace):** One of the 10 Principles; the philosophy of cleaning up absolutely everything you brought in.

### **Slang & Cultural Terms**

* **Sparkle Pony:** A participant who brings elaborate costumes but lacks essential survival gear (water, food, lights), relying on others for basic needs. Often used pejoratively.  
* **Darkwad / Darktard:** A person walking or biking at night without lights, creating a severe safety hazard for art cars and cyclists.  
* **Burgin:** A "Virgin Burner"; someone attending Burning Man for the first time.  
* **Playa Chicken:** A mythical creature often blamed for missing items or mysterious noises; a running joke, as no chickens live on the playa.  
* **Default World:** The rest of the world outside of Burning Man; "normal" life.  
* **Exodus:** The process of leaving the city at the end of the event, often involving multi-hour traffic jams (Pulse).

## **Survival Guide: Common Problems and Solutions**

### **Water and Hydration**

* **Problem:** Dehydration happens faster than realized due to the high altitude (4,000 ft) and extreme aridity.  
* **Rule:** Bring **1.5 gallons** of water per person, per day.  
* **Advice:** Drink before you are thirsty. If your urine is dark, you are already dehydrated. Carry a water bottle everywhere. Electrolytes are mandatory, not optional.

### **Food and Nutrition**

* **Problem:** Appetite suppression is common due to heat and stimulation.  
* **Advice:** Do not rely on cooking complex meals. Bring "grab-and-go" food like pickles (for salt), vacuum-sealed meals, and protein bars.  
* **Gifting Myth:** Do not rely on gifting for food. You must be radically self-reliant.

### **Bikes**

* **Problem:** The city is massive (approx. 7 square miles). Walking everywhere is exhausting.  
* **Advice:** Bring a "beater" bike (cruisers are best). Mountain bikes with gears often clog with dust.  
* **Essential:** You *must* light your bike at night. Front and rear lights, plus wheel lights (el wire or spoke lights) for side visibility.  
* **Maintenance:** Bring spare tubes and chain lube (wax-based dry lube is best to avoid attracting dust).

### **Grey Water**

* **Definition:** "Grey water" is used water from cooking, dishwashing, or showering.  
* **Disposal:** You cannot dump this on the ground (that is MOOPing).  
* **Solution:** Use an evaporation pond (a lined frame where water evaporates in the sun) or pack it out in jugs.

## **Technical Explanation: Swamp Coolers (Evaporative Coolers)**

### **What is a Swamp Cooler?**

A swamp cooler is a DIY air conditioning unit popular on the playa. It uses water evaporation to cool hot, dry air.

### **Physics: Why does it work?**

It relies on the **latent heat of evaporation**. When water transitions from liquid to gas (vapor), it absorbs a significant amount of heat energy from the surrounding air.

* **Condition:** This only works effectively in low-humidity environments (\< 15% humidity). The Black Rock Desert is ideal for this.  
* **Delta T:** A well-built unit can drop the air temperature by 20–30°F.

### **Typical Construction (The "Figjam" Bucket Cooler)**

1. **Chassis:** A 5-gallon bucket.  
2. **Intake:** Holes drilled in the sides, covered with a wicking material (like "Duracool" pad).  
3. **Airflow:** A 12V computer fan (usually high static pressure) mounted in the lid, blowing *out* or *in* (inward blowing is preferred to pressurize the tent/yurt).  
4. **Water:** A submersible 12V pump circulates water from the bottom of the bucket over the pads.  
5. **Power:** Powered by a 12V deep-cycle battery or solar panel.

### **Troubleshooting**

* **High Humidity:** If it rains or is humid, the cooler will stop cooling and just make everything damp.  
* **Dust Clogging:** The wet pads act as an air filter. They will get clogged with dust and may need rinsing mid-week.

## **Geography and Navigation**

### **The Clock and Ring System**

Black Rock City is laid out as a 2/3 circle, navigated using a polar coordinate system.

* **Radial Streets (Clock Face):** Named by time, from **2:00** to **10:00**. The "Man" is at the center point.  
* **Concentric Streets (The Alphabet):** Named alphabetically moving outward from the center.  
  * **Esplanade:** The innermost street facing the open playa.  
  * **A through K (or L):** Named streets. The names change every year based on the art theme (e.g., "Aromatic," "Body," "Cerebral").

### **Key Landmarks**

* **The Man:** The physical effigy located at the geographic center of the playa.  
* **The Temple:** Located at 12:00 in the deep playa (past the Man). A solemn space for grief and reflection.  
* **Deep Playa:** The open desert beyond the 10:00 and 2:00 radial streets and the trash fence.  
* **The Trash Fence:** A pentagonal fence marking the perimeter of the event. It catches windblown trash (MOOP). **Warning:** Do not cross the trash fence.

### **Finding Things**

* **Porta Potties:** Located on radial streets (e.g., 3:00, 9:00) generally between C & D streets and H & I streets. Look for blue solar lights at night.  
* **Ice (Arctica):** Sold at specific stations usually located at 3:00, 9:00, and Center Camp.  
* **Medical:** Main stations are at 3:00 & C, 9:00 & C, and Esplanade & 5:15.

## **Playa Dust: Composition and Safety**

### **What is Playa Dust?**

The Black Rock Desert is a prehistoric lake bed (Lake Lahontan). The "sand" is actually fine alkaline silt.

### **Chemical Composition**

* **Primary Components:** Silica, Gypsum, and Calcium Carbonate.  
* **pH Level:** High Alkalinity (pH \~10). It is chemically similar to bleach or cement powder.

### **Health Effects ("Playa Foot")**

Because the dust is alkaline, it neutralizes the skin's natural acidic barrier (the acid mantle).

* **Playa Foot:** A condition where the skin on the feet cracks, bleeds, and develops chemical burns from prolonged dust exposure.  
* **Prevention:** Wear socks and closed-toe shoes. Never walk barefoot.  
* **Treatment:** Wash feet with a solution of water and **vinegar** (acidic) to neutralize the alkalinity. Apply lotion or oil immediately after.

### **Respiratory Safety**

* **Silica Risk:** Prolonged inhalation of fine silica dust can be harmful.  
* **Whiteouts:** Sudden dust storms with zero visibility.  
* **Protocol:** Always carry a dust mask (N95 is best) and goggles. If caught in a whiteout, stop moving, sit down, and wait for visibility to return to avoid being run over by art cars.

### **Cleaning Equipment (Vinegar)**

Because the dust is basic, normal soap (which is also basic) often fails to clean it effectively. Adding white vinegar to your wash water helps break down the dust on equipment, bikes, and car interiors.
