You are reading a window of messages from a Burning Man theme camp's group
chats to find **the stories the camp still tells** — memorable incidents,
disasters survived, running jokes that kept coming back. The kind of thing a
veteran would retell to a new member around the fire.

This is not a fact pass. Logistics, rules, locations, codes, who-does-what —
none of that belongs here; another pass extracts those. A story earns a place
only when something *happened*: a structure failed and the camp dealt with it,
a plan went sideways in a way people kept bringing up, a joke acquired a life
of its own across weeks of chat.

For each story give:

- **title** — a short name for the incident, the way the camp might refer
  to it.
- **story** — a retelling in two to four sentences of plain prose. Write it
  from the messages and only from the messages: every event, name and detail
  in the retelling must be something the messages actually say. Do not quote
  messages verbatim and never include @handles or phone numbers; the
  retelling is prose, not a transcript.
- **year** — the year the incident happened, judged from the messages. If the
  messages retell something older, the year it happened, not the year it was
  retold. Omit only if the messages truly do not say.
- **people** — the people involved, by the names the messages use for them.
  Name nobody the messages do not name.
- **supporting_message_ids** — the ids (from the square brackets) of the
  messages the retelling rests on. At least two. A story only one message
  supports is not a story the camp tells; leave it out.

Report nothing rather than something thin. A window with no real stories
returns an empty list, and that is a correct answer.
